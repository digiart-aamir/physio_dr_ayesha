Five Stretches for Desk‑Job Back Pain

Simple, evidence-based movements you can do at your workstation to ease lumbar tension — no equipment needed.

🕐 5 min readBy Dr. Ayesha Zia, DPT

These stretches are general wellness guidance. If you have an existing injury or chronic pain, always consult Dr. Ayesha before starting any exercise routine.

Why Does Your Back Hurt at a Desk?

Prolonged sitting compresses spinal discs, tightens hip flexors, and weakens core muscles — a triple threat that leads to the familiar dull ache in the lower back. The good news: targeted micro-movements throughout the day can dramatically reduce pain intensity and prevent long-term damage.

01

Cat-Cow Stretch

Sit at the edge of your chair, hands on knees. Inhale and arch your lower back (cow). Exhale and round it toward the chair back (cat). Hold 3 seconds each.

3 × 10 reps · Every 2 hours
02

Seated Spinal Twist

Sit tall, cross your right leg over left. Twist to the right, placing your left hand on your outer right knee. Hold 20 seconds each side.

2 × 20 sec · Each side
03

Hip Flexor Stretch

Sit on the chair edge, slide your right leg back until you feel a pull in your front hip. Keep your torso upright. Switch legs after 30 seconds.

30 sec each side · 3×/day
04

Chest Opener

Clasp hands behind your back. Straighten arms, lift chest and draw shoulder blades together. Counteracts the forward slump position of typing.

Hold 15 sec · Every hour
05

Neck Lateral Stretch

Drop your right ear toward your right shoulder. Use your right hand to gently add a small amount of pressure. Relax your left shoulder down.

Hold 20 sec · Each side

Pain persisting after 2 weeks of stretching?

Stretching helps mild tension, but structural back pain, disc issues or nerve pain need professional assessment. Dr. Ayesha offers a personalised physiotherapy plan built specifically for your posture and daily lifestyle.

What to Expect at Your First Hijama Session

A complete walkthrough of the process, what you'll feel, and how to care for yourself afterwards — so you arrive prepared and confident.

🕐 7 min readBy Dr. Ayesha Zia, DPT

All hijama at Prime Care is performed using sterile, single-use cups and lancets in a private setting. Every session follows Islamic hygienic standards and clinical protocols.

What Is Hijama?

Hijama (حجامہ) is the Islamic practice of wet cupping — an ancient form of medicine endorsed by the Prophet ﷺ and backed by a growing body of modern research for pain relief, blood circulation, and detoxification. Dr. Ayesha combines the Sunnah method with clinical physiotherapy knowledge to deliver safe, effective sessions.

The Session — Step by Step

1

Consultation (10–15 min)

Dr. Ayesha reviews your health history, discusses your goals and identifies the correct cupping points for your condition. Wet or dry cupping is decided at this stage.

2

Dry Cupping (5–10 min)

Sterile cups are placed on the skin and gentle suction is created. This draws blood to the surface and warms the tissue. You'll feel a pulling sensation — not painful.

3

Incisions (1–2 min)

For wet cupping, tiny superficial scratches are made with a sterile lancet. This is the part most people are anxious about — in reality it feels like a mild scratch and is over in seconds.

4

Wet Cupping (5–10 min)

Cups are reapplied. A small amount of blood is drawn out — this is believed to remove stagnant blood and metabolic waste from the tissues.

5

Aftercare Application

The area is cleaned with antiseptic. Black seed oil (kalonji) is often applied — a Sunnah aftercare practice with antimicrobial properties.

Aftercare — What To Do

✓ Do

  • Rest for the remainder of the day
  • Keep the cupping sites dry for 24 hours
  • Drink plenty of warm water
  • Eat light, wholesome food
  • Apply black seed oil as instructed

✗ Avoid

  • Cold showers or swimming for 24h
  • Heavy exercise on the day of cupping
  • Tight clothing over cupping sites
  • Seafood on the day (traditional precaution)

Walking After a Cardiac Event — How Much Is Safe?

A practical, evidence-based guide to pacing your recovery walks in the weeks after a heart attack, bypass or angioplasty.

🕐 6 min readBy Dr. Ayesha Zia, DPT

Always follow your cardiologist's specific instructions first. This article provides general guidance. Dr. Ayesha will tailor your walking plan after a full cardiac assessment.

Why Walking Is the Best First Exercise

Walking is low-impact, adjustable, and directly trains the cardiovascular system without overloading it. Research from the European Society of Cardiology shows that structured walking within 2 weeks of a cardiac event significantly improves long-term outcomes, reduces re-hospitalisation risk, and improves mood — a crucial factor in recovery.

The 6-Week Progressive Plan

Week 1–2

Gentle Mobilisation

5–10 minute walks, flat terrain only, twice daily. Stop if you feel breathless, chest tightness, or your heart rate exceeds the limit set by your cardiologist.

⏱ 5–10 min🚶 Flat ground📊 Very light effort
Week 3–4

Building Endurance

Extend walks to 15–20 minutes. You should be able to hold a conversation comfortably throughout. Gentle inclines only if Week 1–2 went well.

⏱ 15–20 min🚶 Light terrain📊 Light effort
Week 5–6

Moderate Activity

Target 25–30 minutes, once or twice daily. You may introduce slightly brisker pacing if you've been symptom-free. Always warm up and cool down for 3 minutes.

⏱ 25–30 min🚶 Mixed terrain📊 Moderate effort

Warning Signs — Stop Immediately If:

⚠️Chest pain, pressure or tightness
⚠️Severe shortness of breath at rest pace
⚠️Dizziness, lightheadedness or fainting
⚠️Irregular or unusually rapid heartbeat
⚠️Cold sweats or sudden nausea
